## Problem Statement

OmniSupport, a retail customer support operation, generates roughly 1 million support tickets annually across its channels. Two operational pain points motivate this project. First, support managers currently have no reliable way to anticipate which incoming tickets will take unusually long to resolve, which limits their ability to proactively allocate agent capacity. Second, tickets that escalate to specialist queues are only identified as escalations after the fact, meaning senior agents are pulled in reactively rather than being routed the highest-risk tickets from the outset. This project builds two predictive models, a resolution-time regressor and an escalation classifier. Both scored at the moment a ticket is created, so that staffing and routing decisions can be made proactively rather than reactively.

## Target 1: Resolution Time (Regression)

**Target variable:** resolution_time_hours
**Prediction timing:** At ticket creation, using only information available at that point (customer/order metadata, channel, initial category, priority as assigned at intake).
**Business decision supported:** Flagging tickets predicted to take unusually long, so they can be prioritised for early agent attention rather than sitting in a standard queue.
**Success metric:** MAE and RMSE, interpreted in business terms as "average hours of error in a staffing/capacity forecast." Given the business use case is prioritisation rather than precise time-tracking, MAE is the primary metric since it's more interpretable to a non-technical stakeholder than RMSE.

## Target 2: Escalation (Classification)

**Target variable:** escalated
**Prediction timing:** At ticket creation, same feature-availability constraint as above.
**Business decision supported:** Proactively routing tickets with high predicted escalation risk to senior agents, rather than waiting for a junior agent to escalate manually.
**Success metric:** Given escalation is expected to be a minority class, accuracy is not an appropriate primary metric. PR-AUC and F1 on the escalated class will be used instead, alongside a probability-threshold table (showing, for a range of cutoffs, what percentage of tickets get flagged and what percentage of true escalations are caught) so the business can choose a threshold that matches its capacity to handle flagged tickets.

## Leakage / Prediction-Timing Audit

The following columns are excluded from the feature set for both models, since each is only known after the ticket outcome has occurred and would not be available at prediction time in production:

- resolution_status_after_7d — a post-resolution follow-up status, recorded after the ticket is closed.
- final_agent_disposition — the agent's closing notes/classification, only assigned at resolution.
- refund_amount — only populated once a refund decision has been made, which happens during or after ticket handling.
- csat_score — a customer satisfaction score collected after the ticket is resolved.
- customer_sentiment — derived from a post-contact review process, so by definition postdates ticket creation.

Additionally, resolution_time_hours is excluded from the feature set for the escalation classifier. Although it is not a post-outcome field relative to itself, it is generated by the same underlying process as escalation (both are outcomes of how a ticket is handled) and is not known at ticket-creation time, so including it would leak information about how the ticket was actually resolved into a model meant to predict escalation risk before that resolution takes place.

## Risks and Assumptions

- **Synthetic data may not reflect real-world ticket text variability.** The dataset's templated phrase structure (confirmed in the Phase 8 NLP audit) makes text classification and RAG retrieval easier than they would likely be on genuine, more varied customer language. Reported classifier and RAG accuracy should be read as an upper bound for this specific data generation process, not a guaranteed real-world result.
- **Escalation model performance is moderate, not high, and weakest on lower-priority tickets.** Given the escalation classifier's F1 of 0.557 (escalated class), a meaningful share of true escalations will not be caught at any reasonable threshold. This is an accepted trade-off for a triage/prioritization tool, but the model should not be presented to the business as a definitive escalation predictor, only as a risk-ranking signal to support human decision-making.
- **The £100 agent-approval threshold and the RAG human-review threshold are both fixed, business-chosen constants.** Neither adapts to individual customer risk profile, order value, or context, and both should be reviewed and owned as ongoing business policy decisions, not treated as permanently fixed by this implementation.
- **Subgroup fairness has only been assessed for customer region.** Other potentially sensitive attributes (e.g. age band, language, accessibility needs) have not been analyzed for differential model performance, and should be reviewed before any production deployment.
- **The knowledge base and agent tools reflect a fixed snapshot.** Policy documents and order/customer data do not update in real time within this project; a production deployment would need a defined process for keeping the RAG knowledge base and agent-accessible data current, and for versioning changes to both.
